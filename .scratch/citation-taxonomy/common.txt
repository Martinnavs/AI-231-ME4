
BACKGROUND. We build a spoken voice assistant (a small local router model plus Gemini 3.1 flash-lite with Google Search grounding, plus Tavily search). The user speaks a question; the answer is spoken in one to three short sentences. The class rule (from the professor) is: for search and fact-finding, be grounded every time, get a source and ensure reliability first; if you do not know or confidence is very low, say so and ask. Our owner wants: for every fact that can change, look it up, double check it and cite the source, never rely on the model's training memory.

QUESTION FOR YOU. Is there an existing TAXONOMY (a published classification, a benchmark's categories, a documented industry practice or a standard) of which questions or claims require retrieval and a citation, and how do deployed systems decide? We want to replace our guess (one regex for roles, "latest", prices, results) with something grounded in the literature.

WHAT WE ALREADY HAVE (do not report these as new):
- OnIt (the professor's agent, github.com/sibyl-oracles/onit): its prompt says "Anything that can change needs a tool result, not memory: a price, a version, a head count, a ranking, a policy, who holds a job"; prefer primary sources; when two sources disagree lead with the better-sourced and more recent one and say the other exists; call an undated figure undated; end with a References section. Its post-answer fact check compares the answer with the evidence the run gathered (verify.py).
- Ours: one regex for changeable questions (roles such as CEO/president/mayor, latest/last/next/current/new, price/rate/version/release/ranking/record/score/result) that gates a retry-with-search, then Tavily, then a hedge; the answer names the source site, two sites when two back it, "Only one source found" otherwise.

WHAT TO REPORT. For each taxonomy or practice you find: its name, authors/year, the categories (list them all), the definition of each category, one example question per category, which categories require a citation or retrieval, how the category is detected (classifier, rules, LLM prompt, a score threshold), and a URL. Then a section "Proposed taxonomy for our assistant": a table with columns category | example | needs source? (always / if changeable / never) | staleness class | how to detect cheaply (regex or a tiny classifier) | evidence (which source above). Then "What we should NOT adopt and why". Mark every claim you could only see in a search snippet as SNIPPET and every claim you read on the page as READ. Do not invent papers; if you cannot find something, say NOT FOUND. Quote category names exactly as the source writes them. Keep the final answer under 1,800 words.
